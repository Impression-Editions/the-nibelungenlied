About the Translator

Daniel B. Shumway (1868⁠–⁠1940) was a distinguished American scholar and philologist whose career was defined by his profound commitment to making the foundational texts of Germanic literature accessible to English-speaking audiences. A long-time professor of Germanics at the University of Pennsylvania, Shumway dedicated his life to the rigorous study of Middle High German, bridging the gap between academic scholarship and literary appreciation. His work remains a cornerstone for students of medieval European literature, characterized by a meticulous attention to the linguistic nuances and the heroic spirit of the original manuscripts.

In this definitive translation of The Nibelungenlied, Shumway captures the raw power and tragic scope of the medieval epic, preserving the rhythmic intensity of the original verse while navigating the complex cultural history of the Burgundian court. Beyond this monumental translation, his scholarly contributions⁠—including extensive research into the development of the German language and its heroic traditions⁠—have provided essential context for understanding the evolution of the “German Iliad.” Through his careful rendering of this anonymous masterpiece, Shumway ensures that the dramatic saga of Siegfried, Kriemhild, and the fall of the Burgundians continues to resonate with modern readers.
